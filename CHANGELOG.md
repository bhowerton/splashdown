# Changelog

What changed in each release of Splashdown. Downloads are on the [releases page](https://github.com/bhowerton/splashdown/releases).

## 0.13.1 — 2026-11-11

- **Windows: the app no longer stops at launch on most PCs.** The speech library inside the Windows app was built for the processor of whichever machine built the release. 0.12.1 was built on one with AVX-512, so on a PC without it (most of them) the app stopped with "illegal instruction" as soon as it started. It's now built for what nearly every PC since 2013 has (AVX2), and on an older processor Splashdown does without on-device speech and says so, where it would have crashed.
- **Setup asks nothing.** The setup guide used to open with a choice between running on your computer and using an API key, and downloaded nothing until you answered. Now one button starts it: Splashdown downloads what it needs and then works on this computer, without the internet. An API key is still there, behind a small link. If there's only room for speech, it takes speech and says so; on a computer too slow to tidy your words up, it types them as you say them and tells you, where it used to suggest a cloud service.
- **One thing per screen, with pictures.** The microphone, then the Accessibility switch (with a picture of the switch to turn on, and help if it's taking a while), then "Say hello": one try, with a picture of which key to hold and two live signs, that the key is held and that the microphone hears you, so a try that fails says why. The last screen shows where Splashdown lives: its icon in the menu bar, or by the clock on Windows, with how to keep it in sight. The other things to try are an optional tour.
- **Closing setup keeps your place.** Open it again and it carries on from the same screen, and until it's finished the menu has "Finish setting up…" at the top, where it used to show a command to type.
- **The Mac disk image says what to do, and Splashdown can move itself.** The window that opens has an arrow and "Drag Splashdown to Applications". If Splashdown is opened from the disk image, Downloads or the Desktop anyway, it offers to move itself to your Applications folder, so it can update itself and start when you log in.
- **A vocabulary your team shares.** One list of names, terms and corrections for a whole team, kept as a file in a folder you already share (Dropbox, Google Drive, OneDrive, iCloud Drive or Box). In Settings → Words & language → Your team's words, Start a team list… makes the file and copies an invite link; a teammate clicks the link, sees what the list has, and joins with one click. Its words are used along with your own, yours first, and stay in the file: when someone adds a name, everyone has it within half a minute. The first 300 words and 300 corrections are used. No server and no accounts. See [Splashdown for teams](docs/TEAMS.md).
- **Organizations can set some settings.** On computers an organization manages, a configuration profile (macOS) or Group Policy (Windows) can turn on local-only mode, turn history off, choose the shared vocabulary, and decide how updates arrive. Settings shows each as "Set by your organization". Splashdown still has no server and no accounts. New and not yet tried with a real management tool; see [Splashdown for teams](docs/TEAMS.md).
- **Dictate a draft and keep working on it by voice.** Hold the dictation key and press D: what you say opens in a panel and isn't typed. Keep talking to add to it, or tap the instruction key first to change it ("make the second part shorter", "change every Tuesday to Thursday"). Every version is kept, each saying what made it, and Insert puts the one you want where you were. The letter can be changed in Settings → General.
- **The draft panel tells adding from changing by key.** In the panel Compose drafts open in, speaking used to always be a change. Now a plain dictation adds its words and the instruction key makes a change, the same as everywhere else, and a change that only swaps words is made exactly.
- **"Change every Tuesday to Thursday" changes exactly that.** When what you say about a selection, or about your last dictation, is a plain replacement or removal, Splashdown now makes it on the computer and leaves every other word alone, where the AI used to rewrite the whole text. It says how many it changed ("Changed 4 · Tuesday → Thursday"), keeps each one's capitals, and the undo shortcut puts the text back. Anything else you ask for is rewritten as before.
- **The dictation pill is shorter and says what it is.** The pill that stays on screen for clicking instead of holding a key showed five dots, which didn't say what it was for, and stood taller than it needed to. It now shows a microphone beside a short sound wave and is about a quarter shorter. Both turn orange under the pointer, and the wave moves with your voice while it listens.
- **A letter pressed straight after Right Option works.** On a Mac, holding the dictation key and pressing N for a note, A to ask or T to translate only worked if you paused before the letter: pressed quickly, the recording stopped and the letter was typed. The letters now count however quickly they follow. (Hands-free's H always did.) With a dictation key that makes ordinary shortcuts with letters, such as Right Ctrl on Windows, a quick Ctrl+N still goes to the app as a shortcut.
- **`splashdown://` links work on Windows.** Links that start or stop dictation, open a note, or invite you to a team's words did nothing on Windows. Splashdown now makes itself the app that opens them, and a click reaches the copy that's already running.
- **A meeting follows a change of microphone.** Plug in a headset mid-meeting, or have the microphone in use disappear, and the recording carries on with the other one, where it used to end. A microphone you chose in Settings is kept while it's connected.
- **A silent microphone is named, and Splashdown offers another.** "Hears nothing" now says which microphone, and after a dictation where it heard nothing at all, Splashdown asks once whether to use another one. Automatic also passes over two devices that are never a microphone (the Zoom and Teams audio devices); Settings marks them.
- **Settings → Notes says plainly where your notes are.** "Notes folder" and "Keep them in" are one row now, Where your notes are kept: every place they could be (each sync service this computer has, and this computer), each with its own icon, and the one they're in marked. Before, the row only listed the places they weren't. With several services, or an account name that's long, the buttons wrap instead of squeezing the label to a word's width.
- **Google Drive is found when it keeps your files on the Mac.** With Google Drive set to mirror files, My Drive is a folder in your home folder, and Splashdown didn't see it, so Settings → Notes offered no Google Drive. It does now.
- **In a narrow Settings window, the tabs along the top no longer overlap each other.**

## 0.12.1 — 2026-11-10

- **Updates can install by themselves.** Settings → General → Updates now has three choices: tell me when there's one (as before, and still what you get unless you change it), install them by themselves, or don't check. Installing by itself waits until a version has been out for two days, and for a moment when Splashdown has been idle for ten minutes with nothing recording, no file being transcribed, no model downloading and none of its windows open. It then restarts, and the menu bar and Settings → About say what it updated to for a day, with a link to what changed. The setup guide offers it.
- **Settings has tabs for Notes and Meetings, and a search.** Everything about notes, watched folders and meetings was under General, which had grown to a third of all settings. They're now two tabs of their own, and Meetings is in four groups: recording, starting one, who said what, and when it ends. The two switches for how Compose drafts moved to Words & language, beside the rest of your writing style. A search field at the top of the sidebar (⌘F) finds a setting by its name, its description or another word for it ("shortcut" finds the hotkeys), and takes you to it.
- **A dictation that lands nowhere is no longer lost.** Some apps don't say whether a text field has focus. There, Splashdown sent the text, said "Inserted", and then put back what you had copied before, so with no field to take it the words were on neither the screen nor the clipboard. Now, unless it sees the field change, the text stays on the clipboard and the message says "Couldn't check it went in · it's on the clipboard too". In those apps what you had copied before is no longer put back after a dictation. On a Mac, places that plainly take no text, like Finder's desktop, now get "No text box — copied to clipboard".
- **The mini bar is a small pill, and setup shows it.** The button that stays on screen for clicking instead of holding a key is now a slim pill of five dots at the bottom middle of the screen, where it was a round button by the right edge. The dots turn orange under the pointer, become the sound of your voice while it listens, and sweep while your words are worked on. The end of a first setup shows it, with a switch to turn it off; it's still in Settings → General, now as Show the dictation pill, can be dragged anywhere, and stays where you put it.

## 0.12.0 — 2026-11-10

- **Coding agents can search what you dictated, if you let them.** A new tool in `splashdown-mcp`, `search_history`, finds your dictations by words or date: "what did I dictate about the invoice last week?". It has a switch of its own, off by default, in Settings → Privacy → Coding agents; allowing notes doesn't allow it, because history holds everything you dictated anywhere. An agent gets when, the app, the preset and the text that was inserted, never what was heard before cleanup or the recording, and it needs history kept.
- **Notes from your iPhone.** The new Splashdown Note shortcut (splashdown.app/iphone) turns what you say on your iPhone into text and saves it to your iCloud Drive; Splashdown on your Mac or PC adds it where dictations into a note go (Inbox or today's note), at the time you said it. Start it from the Action Button, Control Center, the Lock Screen or Siri. Windows needs iCloud for Windows.
- **Keep your notes in a sync service with one click.** Settings → General → Notes shows the notes folder and a button for each of iCloud Drive, Dropbox, Google Drive, OneDrive and Box that this computer has; your notes move to a Splashdown Notes folder there. Once they're in one, a button brings them back to this computer. Notes stay on your computer unless you choose.
- **Hooks.** Settings → Actions → When something happens runs your own script or program, or sends to a web address (Zapier, Make, n8n, a Slack incoming webhook), after a dictation, when a meeting or transcript is finished, or when a note comes from your iPhone. A program gets the text as its input and the details in `SPLASHDOWN_` variables, never through a shell; a web address gets JSON signed with your webhook secret. A hook can also change a preset's dictations before they go in: what your script writes back is inserted, or, if it takes over 2 seconds or fails, what you said. Test tries one before saving. Details and examples: docs/HOOKS.md.
- **Hooks can ask the AI.** A hook can now give a finished meeting, a transcript, a dictation or a note from your iPhone to the AI with an instruction in your own words, and put what it writes in the note, at the end of another note or a file, on the clipboard, or to a web address. No script needed. Ready to use: a follow-up email, what you said you'd do, decisions, key points, study cards, and to-dos from a note said on your iPhone. It uses the AI that follows your instructions (the one on this computer, or your cloud provider: the editor says which), it can only write, and Test shows what it would write for a sample meeting. A hook for meetings or transcripts can be limited to those whose title has a word ("1:1"), and **What ran** lists what your hooks did lately. See [docs/HOOKS.md](docs/HOOKS.md).
- **Hooks on a schedule.** A hook can run at a time you set (every day, on weekdays, or one day a week) with the notes you made in the last day or the last 7 days: meetings with their summaries, transcripts, and other notes that changed. Ask the AI for a weekly review or a daily wrap-up (both are ready under Start from), or give the notes to your own script or a web address. A time that passes while the computer is asleep is caught up on when it wakes.
- **The Inbox says which day each line is from.** The first thing you dictate into the Inbox each day, or send from your iPhone, now comes under a heading for the day. A hook on a schedule reads only the lines from the days it covers.
- **A brief before each meeting (Mac).** A hook can run a few minutes before a meeting on your calendar starts (10, or the number you choose), with the meeting and your notes from the last times you met: the last three meeting notes with the same title or mostly the same people, with their summaries. The ready-made Brief asks the AI what was decided, what you said you'd do and what others said they'd do, and collects the answers in a note called Briefs, under the meeting's title and day. Your own script or a web address can get the same text. All-day events and ones you declined are skipped, each event is briefed once, and a meeting with no earlier notes runs nothing. Your calendar is read on your Mac, only while a hook like this is on; macOS asks first.
- **Hooks tell you when they're done, and run when you say.** A hook that asks the AI can show a small panel when it has written something, with how it starts and a button to open the note; the ready-made weekly review, daily wrap-up and brief do. A hook on a schedule or before a meeting has Run now in Settings, and runs by voice too: "run my weekly review". Run again for the same days, a scheduled hook replaces what it wrote for them.
- **Hook results as reminders or an email draft.** What a hook's AI writes can now go to two more places. In Reminders (Mac): each to-do line becomes a reminder in a list you name (checked when you save the hook) or your default one, with the meeting it came from in its notes, and no due date unless the line has one written as a date; Splashdown asks first if you chose to be asked before reminders are added. In an email draft: it opens in your mail app for you to check and send, to the addresses you typed in the hook or to no one, and is never sent for you; a result too long for a draft goes on the clipboard to paste in. Both are ready under Start from: the follow-up email as a draft, and what you said you'd do as reminders. Nothing said in a meeting can add a recipient or make anything else happen.
- **Join and record a meeting from your calendar (Mac).** Turn on Settings → General → Meetings → Offer to join and record meetings on my calendar, and two minutes before an event with a video-call link starts, a small panel shows it with Join and Record. Join opens the link in your browser or call app, and Record starts the meeting note, named from the event. Links to Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, Webex, GoTo Meeting, Whereby and Jitsi Meet are known; an invitation's text is written by whoever sent it, so only an `https` link to one of those is ever opened. Each meeting is offered once, and not again when the call app starts using the microphone. Off unless you turn it on; your calendar is read on your Mac and nothing about it is sent.
- **A hook asks once before using a cloud AI.** Saving a hook that asks the AI, while a cloud AI follows your instructions, says what will be sent each time it runs and asks you to agree. A hook saved with the AI on this computer asks at its next run if you later switch to a cloud AI, or to another provider. Test asks too, before it sends your own notes.
- **The AI on this computer can summarize a long meeting.** It reads about fifteen minutes of talk at once, and a longer meeting's summary failed with "Too long for this model". A long meeting is now read a part at a time, with notes taken from each, and summarized from the notes. An hour-long meeting takes well under a minute on a recent Mac. Its notes on each part are what people said they'd do and what was decided, in their words, so the summary names who took each thing on and doesn't turn a passing remark into an action item.
- **Carry on a meeting that stopped.** A meeting that ended too soon, or picked up again after a break, no longer needs a second note. Carry on recording (a meeting note's menu in the list, or ⌘K) records more into the same note: the new turns are timed from where the note stopped, a line marks where it picked up, and the summary is written again from all of it. If the note keeps its recording, the new part is joined to it, so it still plays from any turn and can be transcribed again as one. It works for a meeting and for a recording of the microphone. Speakers in the new part aren't told apart on their own; Transcribe again does that for the whole recording.
- **The bar above a meeting that's recording reads properly in a narrower window.** "Transcribed on this computer" was squeezed to a word a line beside the levels. It now has a line of its own when there isn't room.
- **Folders in sync services are named as you know them**, "Google Drive › Splashdown Notes" rather than the path macOS keeps them at.
- **Importing a settings file can't run anything unasked.** A settings file can carry hooks and your own actions, which run programs. Importing one now lists what it would run or send your words to (a custom AI server that isn't on this computer included) before you agree, and each of them asks before its first run here, as does any Shortcut the file had on "Always allow". Before, they ran without asking.
- **A meeting's summary no longer goes missing.** When a summary was written, Splashdown also checked whether its instruction was a spoken command ("read this to me", "email this"), and when the AI wrongly said so, the summary was thrown away and the note got none. A saved transform, like the meeting summary or one picked from the menu, is no longer checked that way, which also makes each one a little quicker.
- **Fixes from a review of the whole app.**
  - The log no longer holds what a spoken command said or what the AI wrote for it: only which kind of command it was.
  - A settings file that can't be read (a typo after editing it by hand) is kept as `config.toml.bad` and Splashdown tells you, instead of quietly starting over and then replacing your presets and vocabulary. Settings, spending and Splashdown's other files are saved whole, so a crash or a full disk can't leave half of one.
  - Return works straight after a dictation that couldn't start ("Microphone unavailable", a password field). It was swallowed for up to 30 seconds.
  - A dictation started just after you corrected a word is no longer thrown away. Learning the word applied new settings in the middle of the recording.
  - "Start hands-free" said as a voice action can be finished with the dictation key and canceled with Esc. Nothing stopped it before.
  - Find and replace keeps "$": replacing "five bucks" with "$5" inserted nothing.
  - Long recordings no longer lose bits of audio while the live words show, and a microphone on one input of a many-input audio interface is recorded at its full level.
  - A hook's script is stopped at its time limit even when it leaves something running, and what it started is stopped with it.
  - A slow speech model can no longer end a meeting with "Nobody has spoken for 10 minutes" while people are talking.
  - A cloud speech model's cost for a meeting is counted in your spending.
  - If a meeting's recording can't be saved with its note (a full disk), it's kept, and Splashdown offers to save it when it next starts. It was deleted.
  - Finishing a meeting after Splashdown closed on it no longer adds turns twice.
  - "Transcribe again" keeps the transcript as it was in the note's versions.
  - Watched folders: a file Splashdown closed on before transcribing it is picked up again at the next start.
  - Mac: your clipboard comes back after two dictations in quick succession. The second could leave the first dictation there instead.
  - Windows: an app that's slow to paste no longer gets what was on your clipboard instead of the dictation.
  - Claude Sonnet 5.5 works for cleanup (every request failed), with its cost counted right, and a busy Anthropic server is tried again.
  - The setup guide no longer undoes the models it just set up when you turn voice actions on.
  - The reading Speed slider in Settings saves.
  - Voice actions: text you have selected can't steer "open website" or a Shortcut by sharing a word with what you said, and selected or on-screen text can't pass itself off as your instruction.
  - Local-only mode no longer takes a server address written to look like this computer.
  - A dictation whose key release never arrived (the screen locked, or a password prompt or an administrator's window took the keyboard) ends once the key is up, instead of recording on. Any recording also stops after 3 minutes of silence or 10 minutes, held or hands-free.
  - Windows: the first press of the dictation key after such a missed release is no longer ignored.
  - Stopping a reading with Esc no longer does its work inside the key listener, which Windows can drop for being slow, leaving the dictation key dead until a restart.
  - A built-in model that hangs is given up on after 5 minutes instead of holding dictation up for good, and a very long reply is no longer cut off by the model's own idle timer.
  - A damaged video file that reports no sample rate no longer stalls the transcription queue.
  - Long dictations in Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Thai and other scripts that aren't Latin are no longer cut short by the AI pass.
  - Snippets: a trigger said at the start of a sentence works in any alphabet ("Мой адрес"), and a trigger in Chinese, Japanese or Thai is found inside a sentence.
  - A model download that finished but couldn't be put in place isn't downloaded again, and one carried on later is never joined to a file that was replaced in the meantime.
  - Settings: what you're typing in a preset, an API key, a writing style or a watched folder is no longer wiped when a save finishes a moment later.
  - Notes: Move to the Trash deletes the note it asked about, not one that opened behind the question.

## 0.11.0 — 2026-11-08

- **Runs on macOS 12 Monterey.** Splashdown needed macOS 13 before. On Monterey, dictation, commands, Compose, reading aloud, Notes and transcribing files, links and the microphone all work; Shortcuts actions need macOS 13, and recording meetings or an app's sound macOS 14.2, as before. The 2013 Mac Pro isn't supported.
- **One-on-one calls remember the other person too.** With Recognize people you've named on, a call with one other person now keeps their voice as well: rename "Them" in the transcript and they're named by voice in later calls. Before, only meetings with two or more other people had voices kept.
- **Recognize people you've named turns on Tell the other people apart**, which it needs, and turning that off turns Recognize off.
- **Fewer turns in the wrong language.** With your spoken language on Automatic, a short turn of a meeting or a transcribed app that came out in another language (Hindi letters in an English call, say) is transcribed again in the recording's language: your computer's at first, then the one actually spoken once there's enough to tell. Long turns are kept as heard, so a conversation in two languages stays as it was. Setting your language in Settings → Words & language avoids the guessing altogether.
- **The offer to record a call says it's Splashdown's**, with the app's icon and name, and says more before you click: the calendar event it's for (with calendar naming on), what recording will do with your settings ("Transcribed on this computer · speakers told apart · recording kept", with Change… to Settings), and a reminder to let people know you're recording. A bar shows how long it stays up. "Don't offer for Zoom" now leaves out only that app (Settings → Meetings lists the ones left out, with Offer for all again), and the offer to stop says how long was recorded.
- **Forgetting a person removes every trace of their voice.** Forget in Settings removed them from the people you've named, but their voiceprints stayed with the meetings they were in.
- **Renaming a speaker twice saves their voice once.** Naming Speaker 2 "Dan" and then correcting it to "Dana" kept the voice under both names.
- **Recording an app's sound or the microphone isn't taken for a call.** A call ending while a podcast or lecture was recording offered to stop that recording.
- **Subtitles keep your corrections.** A transcript you edited (a misheard name put right) exported its subtitles as first heard.
- **Windows: the computer's language is its display language**, not its regional format, so English Windows with German dates no longer expects German in meetings.
- **Windows: Settings says Windows things.** Alt instead of Option, Ctrl and Win instead of ⌃ and ⌘, the Recycle Bin, Windows Credential Manager, and Permissions without the Mac-only Accessibility step.
- **The live transcript scrolls during a meeting.** It grew past the window instead, so earlier turns couldn't be reached. In a narrow Notes window, the transcript and My notes now stack, each scrolling on its own.

## 0.10.0 — 2026-11-06

- **Translate as you dictate.** Hold the dictation key and press T, then speak: your words come out in the language picked in the menu bar under Translate into, with the overlay showing "→ Spanish". Press T again for the next of your languages; the overlay says which, and the menu bar follows. It uses the preset the app would use, so its tone and instructions still apply. The first time, pick the language from a short list that appears; it's kept for next time. Choose up to five in Settings → Words & language to switch between them in the menu bar; the letter can be changed in Settings → General.
- **Reply in their language** (off until you turn it on in Settings → Words & language). In email and chat apps, when the conversation on screen is clearly in another language than you speak, what you dictate is written in theirs; the bar says "→ German (their language)" while you speak. It reads only the messages, not the app's labels or a quoted thread, and does nothing when the conversation is mixed or too short to tell.
- **Translate selected text.** Select text, hold the dictation key and press T: it's translated at once. In your own text it's replaced by the translation. In text you can't edit, like a web page or an email you received, the translation opens in the Ask panel, which can read it aloud.
- **⌃⌥T** (Ctrl+Alt+T on Windows) does both without holding a key: it translates the selection, or with nothing selected starts a hands-free dictation that comes out translated (⌃⌥T again to finish).
- **Better translations.** Translating, with the translate key or a preset's "Translate into", now keeps names, product terms, code and links as they are, keeps your tone and formatting, and translates questions and requests instead of answering or carrying them out. A reply far longer or shorter than a translation would be (a model that wrote the poem you dictated, say) is caught, and your words go in as you said them, marked "not translated".
- **Subtitles.** Save a transcript or a meeting as SRT or VTT subtitles: from a note's menu or ⌘K, "Save subtitles". Captions are a line or two, broken at sentence ends and pauses, with the speaker's name when it changes. A file transcribed on this computer is timed word by word; with cloud speech, or for a meeting, the times are estimated from the transcript. Also as a part of Get Note Text in Shortcuts, and `splashdown-cli transcribe-file --srt` / `--vtt`.
- **Watched folders.** In Settings → General → Transcribe new files in a folder, add folders whose new audio and video files should be transcribed: a Zoom recordings folder, a voice recorder's imports. Each new file becomes a transcript note once it has finished copying, in the notes folder you choose, and can get its subtitles saved beside it (`talk.mp4` → `talk.srt`). Files already there when you add the folder are left alone, with a button to transcribe them too; nothing is done twice, and files that arrive while Splashdown is closed are done when it starts.
- **Recognize people you've named in meetings** (Mac; off until you turn it on in Settings → General → Meetings, under "Tell the other people apart"). Name a speaker once (Speaker 2 → Priya) and they're named in later meetings when their voice clearly matches; anyone else stays Speaker 1, 2. When the meeting's calendar event says who was invited, only those people are considered. Voiceprints stay on this computer, never in notes; Settings lists the people known by voice, with Forget. The first time downloads NVIDIA's TitaNet model (about 38 MB).
- **Shortcuts can work with your notes (Mac).** Five more actions:
  - **Find Notes** by what they say, kind (meetings, transcripts…) and time.
  - **Get Note Text**: a whole note, or a meeting's summary, your own notes from it, or its transcript.
  - **Ask My Notes**: a question answered from your notes.
  - **Transcribe File**: an audio or video file into a new note.
  - **Open Note** in Splashdown.

  Settings → Privacy → Shortcuts → "Let shortcuts find and read notes" turns it off. A note hidden from coding agents is hidden from shortcuts too.

## 0.9.0 — 2026-11-05

- **A dictation no longer starts with the one before it.** When you dictate into an app you dictated into a few minutes earlier, the AI sees that earlier text for reference. Smaller models sometimes wrote it out again in front of the new text. Splashdown now takes it off, even while text is typed as it streams in.
- **Splashdown in Shortcuts (Mac).** Six actions to use in your own shortcuts:
  - **Dictate Text** passes what you said on to the next action, cleaned up the way Splashdown types it.
  - **Add to Note** adds text to your note and passes the note on.
  - **Record a Voice Note**, and **Start or Stop Dictation**.
  - **Start Meeting** and **Stop Meeting**, which can wait for the finished note.

## 0.8.0 — 2026-11-04

- **Coding agents can go through your meetings.** With notes allowed for coding agents (Settings → Privacy), `splashdown-mcp` can now list your notes by date, kind, folder or tag ("recap my meetings this week", "what's tagged #launch since Monday?"), with each meeting's summary in the same answer. It can read just a meeting's summary, your own notes from it, or its transcript, from one time to another or for one speaker, and long transcripts come in pages instead of being cut off at 50,000 characters. A search match in a transcript says when it was said. Results link back to the note in Splashdown.
- **Meetings and transcripts have their own switch for agents:** "…including meetings and transcripts", since they hold other people's words. It stays on if you'd already allowed notes, and starts off when you first allow them.
- **Hide from coding agents** in a note's ⋯ menu keeps that note out of every agent's reach.
- **Two ready-made requests for agents:** "Recap my meetings" and "My open action items" come with `splashdown-mcp` as prompts, which Claude Code offers as commands.
- **Set up a coding agent from Settings.** Settings → Privacy → Coding agents → Set up an agent lists Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, VS Code and Claude Desktop with whether Splashdown is set up in each, and a Set up button: Splashdown runs Claude Code's or Codex's own command for you, or opens Cursor's or VS Code's install link, or copies the lines Claude Desktop needs. `splashdown-mcp` now comes with the app (before, you had to build it), and does the same from a terminal: `splashdown-mcp install claude-code`, `agents`, `uninstall`.
- **`splashdown-cli` comes with the app.** The commands that control the running app and its settings (`toggle`, `listen`, `note`, `notes`, `status`, `settings`, `history`, `words import`) work without building anything; Settings → About shows where it is. The pipeline, model and eval commands stay in the full version you build from source.
- **`splashdown-cli notes list`, `search` and `read`** do the same from a terminal or a script: `splashdown-cli notes list --when this_week --kind meeting --summary`.

## 0.7.1 — 2026-11-04

- **Dictating with no text field selected.** When the cursor isn't in a text field (a web page outside any box, a list of files), the text is left on the clipboard and "No text box — copied to clipboard" shows, instead of "Inserted" with the words gone. The same happens when a paste into a browser field doesn't show up.

- Spoken commands in any language: tap Right Shift first, then say "delete that last sentence", "make that more formal" or "send it".
- Hands-free (hold the key and press H), whisper mode for speaking quietly, live text while you speak, and mixed-language dictation.

**Presets**
- Default, Email, Texting, Work chat, Code & Terminal and Notes, matched by app, window title or website. Each sets tone, cleanup, language, translation, its own AI model, auto-send and a shortcut.

**Providers and privacy**
- Your own OpenAI, OpenRouter or Anthropic key with a monthly spending limit, or fully on your computer: Apple's on-device models, or local Whisper plus the built-in local model. Ollama and LM Studio work too.
- When a cloud provider fails, the dictation is retried on your computer.
- No servers, no account, no telemetry. History is off by default.

- History, snippets, a vocabulary that learns from your corrections, Insights, a scratchpad, a mini bar, Undo AI edit, `@file` mentions for coding agents, a CLI, `splashdown://` links and an MCP server.
