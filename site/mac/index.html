Three steps

  1. 1

    Open the file you downloaded

    It's called Splashdown-mac.dmg and it's in your Downloads folder. Double-click it.

  2. 2

    Drag Splashdown onto Applications

    A window opens with two things in it. Hold the Splashdown icon, move it onto the Applications folder, and let go.

  3. 3

    Open Splashdown

    Open it from your Applications folder, or find it in Launchpad, or press ⌘ Space and type Splashdown.

What happens the first time

It sets itself up to run on your computer

A short setup guide opens. It gets Splashdown ready to run on your computer, so nothing you say leaves it. That means downloading about 3 GB. Speech comes first, so you can start dictating after about half a gigabyte, and the rest carries on downloading while you work.

It asks for two permissions

Microphone, so it can hear you. It only listens while you hold the key.

Accessibility, so it can notice the key you hold and type your words into the app you're in.

When your Mac asks, switch on Splashdown. The setup guide ticks each one off as you go.

Where to find it afterwards

Look for the icon at the top right of your screen

Splashdown lives in the menu bar, the strip across the top of the screen, near the clock. Click its icon to open the menu and settings. To dictate, hold Right Option, say something, and let go.

Something's not right?

I opened Splashdown from the disk image window

Close it. Splashdown isn't installed until you drag it onto Applications (step 2). Then open it from Applications.

It's asking for Accessibility

Open System Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Accessibility, and switch on Splashdown. If it's already on and Splashdown still asks, switch it off and on again.

It says nothing can be heard

In System Settings, under Privacy & Security, then Microphone, switch on Splashdown.
