Talk the way you talk. Get the text you meant.

Works in every app

Email, Slack, Messages, docs, the browser, your terminal or code editor. Hold the key wherever the cursor is.

Cleans up, doesn't rewrite

The "um"s, false starts and do-overs disappear, punctuation appears, and your words stay yours. Want it exactly as said? Hold the key and press 2.

Commands in any language

"Make that more formal." "Delete that last sentence." "Envíalo." Say what you want however you'd phrase it; there's no list of magic words.

A style for every app

Presets switch on their own: casual in Messages, polished in email, exact in your terminal. Names in the chat on screen come out right, and a name you fix once is learned.

On your computer or in the cloud

Run fully offline with Apple's on-device models or the built-in local model, or use your own OpenAI, OpenRouter or Anthropic key with a spending limit.

Free and open source, for good

No subscription, no word limits, no account. The code is on GitHub under MIT or Apache-2.0, and every release is signed.

Say what to do, not just what to write.

Open, search, play

"Open Spotify." "Search YouTube for paella recipes." "Go to GitHub." "Pause the music." "Set the volume to 30." In any language, however you'd phrase it.

Reminders, events and to-dos

"Remind me at 5 to stretch." "Add dentist to my calendar on Thursday at 4." "Add milk to my to-dos." Changed your mind? "Undo that."

Your own actions

Give a link, a script or keys to press a name, and say it: "deploy staging", "next slide", "open issue 42". Splashdown shows exactly what will run and asks first, until you choose Always allow.

Nothing sent, deleted or bought

An email opens as a draft for you to send. Shortcuts and your own actions ask before they run. Text you've selected can't give orders, and nothing happens while you type a password.

Music steps aside

Whatever's playing is turned down while you speak, as with Siri, and comes back when you let go. Or have it muted or paused.

"What can I say?"

Ask, and a window lists everything you can say or press, with your own keys and actions. The setup guide has you try the main things first, one at a time.

Say what to write, not the words.

"Reply saying I'm in"

Hold the key, tap Right Shift and say what you want. It's written right into your reply, for the app you're in and the conversation on screen, read without selecting it: a greeting and your name in an email, short in a chat.

Sounds like you

It writes the way you do in the conversation, and learns from how you edit its drafts. You can read what it learned, change it, or forget it.

Nothing made up

Names, dates and numbers come only from what you said, the message and your notes; anything missing is left for you to fill in. Hidden instructions in an email are ignored.

Speak your language. Write in theirs.

Hold, tap T, speak

Hold the dictation key, tap T and talk: it comes out in Spanish, French or any of five languages you pick. Tap T again for the next one. The first time, choose from a short list; nothing to set up first.

Reply in their language

Turn it on, and in an email or chat that's clearly in German, what you say is written in German, with no key to press. It reads only the messages, and does nothing when the conversation is mixed or too short to tell.

Translate what you read

Select text and tap T while holding the key. In your own text it's replaced; on a web page or a message you received, the translation opens beside it, and Read aloud says it in that language.

Hear it, too.

"Read this to me"

Select text and say it, and it's read aloud: an email before you send it, a long message, what you just dictated. Esc stops it, and music pauses while it reads.

"Read me a summary of this"

A short summary written for listening, read as it's written. Or "read me the action items". The answer stays in a panel to copy.

A natural voice, on your computer

Phonon reads English in a voice that sounds like a person, with dates and amounts said properly: open source, a small download, quick even on an older PC. Other languages get your system's best voice.

Turn anything you hear into notes.

A call, a webinar in your browser, a podcast, a YouTube video, a lecture in the room: pick the app, and what it says is written into a note as it plays, on your computer.

Any app's audio, as notes

Choose an app (a browser tab's webinar, a podcast player, a call you're only listening to) and only that app's sound is recorded and transcribed. Or the room's microphone, a voice memo, a video file or a YouTube link.

Notes that are just files

Every note is a Markdown file in a folder you choose, so Obsidian sees them too. Keep them in iCloud Drive, Dropbox, Google Drive or OneDrive with one click to have them everywhere, and say a note on your iPhone to add it. Nested folders, #tags, ⌘K to find anything, and formatting as you write.

Meeting notes without a bot

Record your microphone and what the call plays, turn by turn, with your own notes beside the live transcript. Each voice on the call is told apart, and on a Mac the title comes from your calendar. A summary of decisions and action items follows, even for an hour-long meeting with the AI on your computer. Splashdown offers to record when a call starts, saying what it will do and reminding you to let people know, and a meeting that stopped too soon carries on in the same note.

Ask your notes

Hold the key, press A and ask, from any app, or press ⌘J in Notes. Answers come from your notes, found by what they mean as well as their words, and link to the ones they used.

Rewrites you check first

Clean up, summarize, turn into an email or say how in your own words. See exactly what changed, then keep it or try again.

Nothing gets lost

If a provider fails or the app closes mid-sentence, the recording is kept and turned into text when you're ready. Earlier versions of every note are kept too.

Meetings that remember who's who.

Name someone once

After a call, the other people are told apart as Speaker 1, 2 and so on, or Them on a one-on-one call. Pick a name from the invite, and every turn they said takes it.

Known by voice next time

In later meetings, people you've named are recognized by their voice, on a Mac, and only when it's a clear match. Anyone new stays Speaker 1, 2 until you name them.

Voices stay on your computer

It's off until you turn it on. A voiceprint is kept only for people you name, never in your notes and never sent anywhere, and Settings lists them, each with Forget.

It follows up, so you don't have to.

Before a meeting, as it starts, when it ends, at the end of the week: say what you want once, in your own words, and it happens each time.

A brief before each meeting

A few minutes before a meeting on your calendar, on a Mac, the AI reads your notes from the last times you met and says what was decided, what you said you'd do and what others said they'd do.

Join and record in a click

Turn it on, and two minutes before a meeting on your calendar starts, on a Mac, a small card shows it with Join and Record. Join opens its Zoom, Meet, Teams or Webex link, and Record starts the note, named from the event. Each meeting is offered once.

Reminders and a draft after

What you said you'd do becomes reminders in the list you name, and the follow-up email opens as a draft for you to check and send. Or have decisions, key points or study cards put in the note.

A weekly review on Friday

At a time you set, the notes of the week or the day become a review: what was decided, what's still open, what's waiting on others. A panel says when it's ready, or say "run my weekly review".

Your words, or your script

"Draft the follow-up email." "List what I said I'd do." Hooks need no script, and the common ones are ready to start from. Or run a program with the text, or send it to Zapier, Make, n8n or a Slack webhook.

Nothing behind your back

A hook's AI can only write: an email is a draft, never sent, and nothing said in a meeting can add a recipient. It asks once before using a cloud AI, and What ran lists everything your hooks did.

Drop a file, or just a folder.

Watched folders

Choose a folder, like where Zoom saves recordings or your recorder imports to, and every new audio or video file there becomes a transcript note by itself, once it's finished copying. Nothing is done twice.

Subtitles in a click

Save any transcript or meeting as SRT or VTT subtitles, or have them saved beside each watched video. Transcribed on your computer, every caption is timed to the words.

Shortcuts on a Mac

Dictate, add to a note, record a voice note or a meeting, transcribe a file, find and read notes, or ask them a question, as actions in your own shortcuts.

Private by default

Splashdown has no servers, no account and no analytics. Your voice and text go only to the provider you choose, straight from your computer, or nowhere at all with on-device models. API keys stay in your system keychain, history is off unless you turn it on, and the logs never contain what you said. Reading aloud happens on your computer.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. Splashdown is open source and costs nothing. If you use a cloud AI provider, you pay that provider directly with your own key; typical dictation costs cents a month, and you can set a monthly limit. With on-device models it costs nothing at all.

What do I need to run it fully offline?

On an Apple Silicon Mac with macOS 26, Apple's built-in speech and language models. On any other Mac or PC, local speech-to-text (Nemotron writes as you speak) plus Splashdown's built-in local model, a free download with nothing else to install. Local-only mode then refuses every cloud provider. Text is read aloud on your computer too, in a natural voice that's a small download.

Which languages does it understand?

Dozens. It detects the language as you speak, handles switching languages mid-sentence, and can write what you say in another language: tap T while holding the key, have a preset always translate, or let it reply in the language of the conversation on screen. Commands and spoken formatting work in any language too.

How is it different from built-in dictation?

Built-in dictation types your words as spoken. Splashdown also cleans them up, follows instructions ("make it a list"), edits by voice, adapts its style per app, learns your vocabulary, does things when you ask, and reads text back to you.

I'm switching from another dictation app. Can I bring my words?

Yes. Export your dictionary or snippets from the other app, then choose Settings → Words & language → Import… in Splashdown. Your vocabulary, corrections and snippets come along, nothing you already have changes, and there's no word limit waiting for you.

Can it do things on my computer, not just type?

Yes, when you turn on voice actions in Settings → Actions. Hold the dictation key, tap Right Shift and say what to do: open an app, search, control music and volume, add a reminder, event or to-do, draft an email, run a Shortcut on a Mac, or one of your own actions (a link, a script or keys to press). It never sends, deletes or buys anything, and Shortcuts and your own actions ask before they run.

Can it read text to me?

Yes. Select text, hold the dictation key and say "read this to me", or "read me a summary of this" for a short summary written for listening. English is read in Phonon, an open-source voice that sounds like a person and runs on your computer; other languages use your system's best voice. Esc stops it, and music pauses while it reads.

Will it run well on an older or slower PC?

Speech to text runs well almost anywhere. The built-in AI that cleans up your words needs more: it uses the processor's fast instructions, and the graphics chip on Windows where there is one. Setup times it on your computer, and if it's slow, says so and suggests a cloud key, which takes about a second for a few cents a day. You can also turn the AI off and have your words typed exactly as you say them.

Does it stop my music while I talk?

It turns other sounds down while you hold the key, and puts them back when you let go. You can choose mute or pause instead, or leave them be. If your speakers' volume can't be changed from the computer (some audio interfaces), it pauses Spotify and Music on a Mac, or whatever's playing on Windows.

Can it turn a video or podcast playing on my computer into notes?

Yes. In Notes, choose Transcribe, then the app that's playing: a webinar in your browser, a podcast app, a video, a call you're only listening to. Only that app's sound is recorded, and what it says is written into a note as it plays, with time stamps, then a summary if you like. It also takes the room's microphone, audio and video files, and YouTube or podcast links.

Does it record meetings? Does a bot join the call?

No bot joins. Splashdown records your microphone and what your computer plays, on your computer, and writes the conversation into a note as it goes, each turn marked You or Them. It offers to record when a call starts, and on a Mac it can offer a meeting on your calendar two minutes before it starts, with Join and Record. Tell people they're being recorded. With speech-to-text on your computer, nothing leaves it; with a cloud AI, it asks before sending the transcript for a summary.

Can it tell who said what, and remember them?

Yes. At the end of a meeting the other people are told apart (Speaker 1, 2…, or Them on a one-on-one call), and you name them from the invite or by typing. Turn on Recognize people you've named (Mac) and they're named by voice in later meetings when it's a clear match. Their voiceprints stay on your computer, and some places regulate keeping them, so ask people first if you're unsure.

Can it do things by itself, like follow up after a meeting?

Yes, with hooks, in Settings → Actions → When something happens. When a meeting ends, the AI can draft the follow-up email, turn what you said you'd do into reminders, or list the decisions. A hook can also run on a schedule (a weekly review of your notes) or, on a Mac, a few minutes before a meeting on your calendar, with a brief from your last notes about it. Emails open as drafts and are never sent for you, and you can run your own script or send to a web address instead of asking the AI.

A meeting stopped too soon. Do I need a second note?

No. Choose Carry on recording from the meeting note's menu, and more is recorded into the same note: the new turns are timed from where it stopped, the summary is written again from all of it, and a kept recording stays one recording.

Can it make subtitles?

Yes. Any transcript or meeting can be saved as SRT or VTT subtitles from the note's menu, and a watched folder can save them beside each video it transcribes. With speech-to-text on your computer the captions are timed to each word; with cloud speech the times are estimated.

Where are my notes kept?

In a folder you choose (Documents › Splashdown Notes unless you pick another), one Markdown file per note, with folders as real folders. Other apps can open and edit them, and Splashdown picks up their changes. Nothing is kept in a database or a cloud of ours.

Does it work with coding agents?

Yes. File names you say become @file mentions for Claude Code or Cursor, and the splashdown-mcp server lets an agent ask you a question by voice and, if you allow it, search, read and add to your notes, and go through your meetings by date. Searching what you dictated has a switch of its own, off until you turn it on.

Something isn't working.

See troubleshooting, or use Send feedback in the app's menu to open an issue with your versions filled in.
