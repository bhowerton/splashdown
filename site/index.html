Talk the way you talk. Get the text you meant.

Works in every app

Email, Slack, Messages, docs, the browser, your terminal or code editor. Hold the key wherever the cursor is.

Cleans up, doesn't rewrite

The "um"s, false starts and do-overs disappear, punctuation appears, and your words stay yours. Choose how much cleanup each app gets.

Commands in any language

"Make that more formal." "Delete that last sentence." "Envíalo." Say what you want however you'd phrase it; there's no list of magic words.

A style for every app

Presets switch on their own: casual in Messages, polished in email, exact in your terminal. Each can translate, press Return, or use its own AI.

On your computer or in the cloud

Run fully offline with Apple's on-device models or the built-in local model, or use your own OpenAI, OpenRouter or Anthropic key with a spending limit.

Free and open source, for good

No subscription, no word limits, no account. The code is on GitHub under MIT or Apache-2.0, and every release is signed.

Notes, meetings and transcripts, built in.

Notes that are just files

Every note is a Markdown file in a folder you choose, so Obsidian, iCloud and Dropbox see them too. Nested folders, #tags, ⌘K to find anything, and formatting as you write.

Meeting notes without a bot

Record your microphone and what the call plays, turn by turn, with your own notes beside the live transcript. A summary of decisions and action items follows, and Splashdown offers to record when a call starts.

Transcribe anything that plays

A webinar in your browser, a podcast app, a lecture in the room, a voice memo or a YouTube link: pick it, and it's written into a note as it plays.

Ask your notes

Hold the key, press A and ask, from any app, or press ⌘J in Notes. Answers come from your notes and link to the ones they used.

Rewrites you check first

Clean up, summarize, turn into an email or say how in your own words. See exactly what changed, then keep it or try again.

Nothing gets lost

If a provider fails or the app closes mid-sentence, the recording is kept and turned into text when you're ready. Earlier versions of every note are kept too.

Private by default

Splashdown has no servers, no account and no analytics. Your voice and text go only to the provider you choose, straight from your computer, or nowhere at all with on-device models. API keys stay in your system keychain, history is off unless you turn it on, and the logs never contain what you said.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. Splashdown is open source and costs nothing. If you use a cloud AI provider, you pay that provider directly with your own key; typical dictation costs cents a month, and you can set a monthly limit. With on-device models it costs nothing at all.

What do I need to run it fully offline?

On an Apple Silicon Mac with macOS 26, Apple's built-in speech and language models. On any other Mac or PC, local speech-to-text (Nemotron writes as you speak) plus Splashdown's built-in local model, a free download with nothing else to install. Local-only mode then refuses every cloud provider.

Which languages does it understand?

Dozens. It detects the language as you speak, handles switching languages mid-sentence, and can translate into another language per app. Commands and spoken formatting work in any language too.

How is it different from built-in dictation?

Built-in dictation types your words as spoken. Splashdown also cleans them up, follows instructions ("make it a list"), edits by voice, adapts its style per app, and learns your vocabulary.

I'm switching from another dictation app. Can I bring my words?

Yes. Export your dictionary or snippets from the other app, then choose Settings → Words & language → Import… in Splashdown. Your vocabulary, corrections and snippets come along, nothing you already have changes, and there's no word limit waiting for you.

Does it record meetings? Does a bot join the call?

No bot joins. Splashdown records your microphone and what your computer plays, on your computer, and writes the conversation into a note as it goes, each turn marked You or Them. Tell people they're being recorded. With speech-to-text on your computer, nothing leaves it; with a cloud AI, it asks before sending the transcript for a summary.

Where are my notes kept?

In a folder you choose (Documents › Splashdown Notes unless you pick another), one Markdown file per note, with folders as real folders. Other apps can open and edit them, and Splashdown picks up their changes. Nothing is kept in a database or a cloud of ours.

Does it work with coding agents?

Yes. File names you say become @file mentions for Claude Code or Cursor, and the splashdown-mcp server lets an agent ask you a question by voice and, if you allow it, search and add to your notes.

Something isn't working.

See troubleshooting, or use Send feedback in the app's menu to open an issue with your versions filled in.
